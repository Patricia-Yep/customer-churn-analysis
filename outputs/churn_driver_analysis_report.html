Phase 6 · Final report

What actually predicts churn

A synthetic 10,000-customer dataset was screened across five feature categories — profile, product usage, billing, support, and engagement — to find which factors separate churned customers from retained ones. This report documents the cleaning decisions, the per-category test results, and what's out of scope until a multivariate model is built.

01

Cleaning decisions

The raw file (32 columns) was left untouched. All cleaning landed in a separate _cleaned.csv as additive columns — nothing was overwritten, imputed in place, or dropped.

  • Range checks passed with zero violations. nps_score (−100–100), csat_score (1–5, whole values only despite being stored as float), and age (18–74, inside the requested 10–100) all fell cleanly inside range with no sentinel or placeholder values.
  • No duplication. All 10,000 customer_id values are unique; no fully duplicate rows.
  • Text-coded booleans confirmed clean. discount_applied, price_increase_last_3m, and survey_response use consistent Yes/No or category labels — no case, whitespace, or near-duplicate variants.
  • complaint_type_clean added. A copy of complaint_type with its 2,045 missing values (20.45%) labeled "Unknown". The original complaint_type column is untouched.
  • Two mismatch flags added, not resolved. Investigation showed complaint_type's missingness doesn't cleanly mean "no support contact": complaint_missing_with_tickets flags 1,402 rows where the type is missing but ≥1 ticket was logged; complaint_present_no_tickets flags 2,369 rows with a recorded type but zero tickets. Both are boolean columns, left for downstream analysis to interpret rather than resolved by assumption.

04

What csat_score can and can't tell us

The word "outcome" was initially used to describe this finding, which implies csat_score measures satisfaction with a specific resolved support case. That's not supported by what's actually documented.

What we actually know: the dataset's only documentation is a top-level feature-category description that places csat_score inside a bucket labeled "Customer Support: tickets, resolution time, CSAT, complaints." There is no field-level definition of what triggers the score or when it's collected — e.g. a post-ticket survey vs. a periodic relationship check-in.

Revised claim: customers with lower overall satisfaction are more likely to churn. We cannot say dissatisfaction with a specific support resolution drives churn — that would require confirming csat_score is captured per-ticket, which the source data doesn't establish.

05

Next: a multivariate model

Everything above is univariate — each factor was tested against churn on its own, so none of it separates a real independent driver from a factor that just moves alongside one. Two relationships in this dataset make that gap worth closing before acting on the findings:

→
tenure_months ↔ total_revenue. Revenue accrues over the life of the account, so shorter tenure mechanically produces lower lifetime revenue. total_revenue's −21.9% gap may be almost entirely explained by tenure rather than adding independent signal.
→
monthly_logins ↔ last_login_days_ago. Frequency and recency of login are two views of the same underlying engagement behavior and are likely to be strongly correlated with each other — a model may need to pick one or combine them rather than treat both as independent.
→
payment_failures ↔ csat_score. Billing friction plausibly depresses satisfaction (or the reverse); worth testing whether one factor's effect survives once the other is in the model.

Recommended next step: a logistic regression of churn on the five significant factors plus total_revenue, with variance inflation factors (VIF) checked before trusting any individual coefficient. If VIF flags the tenure/revenue or login-frequency/recency pairs, drop or combine the redundant one rather than reporting both as independent drivers. An interaction term for tenure_months × monthly_logins is also worth testing — a newer customer with low logins may be a materially different risk than a long-tenured one with a recent dip.

Reproducing this analysis

The full pipeline — range checks, quality assessment, cleaning, all five category comparisons, and this report's chart — is captured in a standalone script alongside the dataset:

  • churn_analysis.py reads customer_churn_business_dataset.csv and runs end to end with no chat context required — only pandas, scipy, and matplotlib.
  • customer_churn_business_dataset_cleaned.csv is regenerated by the script (35 columns: the original 32 plus complaint_type_clean and the two mismatch flags).
  • churn_drivers_chart.png is regenerated by the script as a static version of the §03 chart above, for use outside this report.
